# Reformulations: the sentences a passage would use to state the answer.
# The idea is AskMSR's: "who sang X" is answered by a sentence that says
# "X was recorded by ..." or "X is a song by ...", so look for those words
# and take what stands in the answer's place. The table itself is this
# library's own.
#
# Lines: question ; question => type :: statement @weight ; statement @weight
#
# Question side, matched like answertypes.txt: lowercased, whole question.
#   {X} {Y} {Z} {R}  bind one or more words of the question
#   A binding loses a leading "the", "a" or "an", and a trailing time phrase
#   ("each year", "a year", "per year", "every year", "annually", "today",
#   "now", "ever"), before it is looked for in a passage.
#
# Statement side, matched against each sentence of the top 20 passages,
# ignoring case:
#   {X} {Y} {Z} {R}  the same words as bound in the question; a leading
#                    "the" in the sentence is allowed and ignored
#   {A}              the answer slot: a span of the type, from spans_of(),
#                    that starts right after the words before it and ends
#                    right before the words after it. Two allowances: the
#                    word before may be a title's own leading "The", and a
#                    number's joined noun or unit may be the next word
#                    ("{A} people visit {X}" takes "two million people")
#   {*}              1 to 6 words of anything, within the sentence
#   Other words      literal, in that order, with nothing between them
#   A statement with no {X} counts only when the sentence also holds every
#   word bound to {X} (in any order), so "recorded by {A}" needs the song.
#   {X} may also be matched by a pronoun (he, she, it, they) that starts the
#   sentence, when the passage's document is the subject's own article or
#   the sentence before it began with {X}.
#
# type: what {A} must be (as in answertypes.txt). person/organization takes
#   either. * keeps the type answer_type() gave the question.
# @weight: 1 to 5, how sure a match is. 5: the sentence says it outright.
#   1: the words are only near each other.
#
# Every line whose question side matches applies, not only the first. A
# sentence counts once per candidate, at its highest matching weight.
